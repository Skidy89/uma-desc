Oguri Cap

About Oguri Cap

Known as the Gray Monster, Oguri Cap became a legendary racehorse loved by fans. Oguri Cap (Japanese: オグリキャップ, Hepburn: Oguri Kyappu; 27 March 1985 – 3 July 2010) was a Japanese Thoroughbred racehorse and stud, sired by Dancing Cap. Oguri Cap was inducted into the Japan Racing Association Hall of Fame in 1991. A horse from the countryside who found success at the national level, he was known as a member of the "Heisei Big Three", alongside Super Creek and Inari One.

Real-Life History (The Gray Monster)

The real Oguri Cap was born on March 27, 1985, at the Inaba Ranch. He was sired by the American stallion Dancing Cap, a descendant of Native Dancer, out of the dam White Narubi. His childhood name was Hatsuratsu, a term meaning "lively" or "energetic". As a newborn foal, he was plagued with a severe right front leg deformity that caused it to turn completely outwards, leaving him unable to stand without ranch staff physically holding him up to nurse. Though corrective hoof trimming fixed his leg in just three days, his mother suffered from poor milk production. Driven by a bottomless hunger, the young foal began eating weeds and even his own toxic straw bedding. Possessing an incredible digestive system, he never fell ill and grew so extraordinarily fast that by the age of one, he boasted the physical build of a two-year-old. He debuted on May 19, 1987, at the regional Kasamatsu Racecourse, where he initially lost to his earliest rival, March Tosho. However, after a new stable attendant discovered and treated severe rotting inside his hooves, Oguri's true potential was unlocked, allowing him to exact revenge on March Tosho and secure 10 wins out of 12 regional races. His tremendous kicking power meant his hooves wore through iron horseshoes every 10 days, much faster than the standard 15-20 days. In January 1988, he was sold to Isoo Sahashi for 25 million yen and transferred to the central Japan Racing Association (JRA).
